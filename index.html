At the bench and in the terminal.

Most problems in crop biology don't sit neatly on one side of that line, so I try not to either.

I'm a plant scientist working where molecular biology meets data science. I develop transgene-free, gene-edited crops and use CRISPR, multi-omics and machine learning to understand how plants grow, age and defend themselves.

At Soilcea, I work on CRISPR-edited citrus rootstocks with resistance to Huanglongbing, the disease behind Florida's citrus decline. That means Agrobacterium transformation and tissue culture on one side, and whole-genome sequencing pipelines that check every edit on the other.

My doctoral work at the University of Florida asked why crops like broccoli decline so fast after harvest, and which genes control it. The question behind both jobs is the same: how do we keep more of what we grow?

I care about reproducible science: open code, documented pipelines and results that hold up.
